---
title: Walking the line to Patching Lake
date: 2026-08-28
excerpt: Three cabins up the Naha country, two of them float-plane only. Here is the route we are flagging and what has to happen before anyone cuts.
---

The Naha River trail already does most of the work. Six miles from tidewater at Naha Bay,
past Roosevelt Lagoon and *Jordan Lake* and the falls, and you are at the Heckman Lake
cabin. That part has been there for decades.

What it does not do is keep going. The Southeast Heckman cabin sits across the water, and
the Patching Lake cabin is another few miles northeast over the divide. Both are float-plane
country. That works right up until the ceiling comes down, and then a booked cabin turns into
a party waiting on weather with no way to walk out.

## What we are looking at

From the end of the mapped tread, around the south shore past the second cabin, then east up
the benches and over a divide that stays under four hundred feet. Not a ridge route — the low
ground. I have been putting it together on foot, and the handheld says a little under five
miles for the Patching leg.

The elevation is the part that surprised people. Straight from Heckman to Patching you cross
a twenty-four-hundred-foot ridge. Swing southeast around it and the whole thing tops out at
about three hundred eighty feet. Same two cabins, a fraction of the climb, and ground a
loaded pack can handle.

## What has to happen first

Nothing gets cut on a hunch. The order is:

Finish the alignment on foot, end to end, and get it off the handheld and onto a real map.
Then an environmental analysis with the Ketchikan–Misty Fjords Ranger District — fish, deer
winter range, wetlands, cultural sites. That review is what tells us where tread is allowed
and where it is not, and it comes before a saw comes out of the truck.

After that it is brushing, puncheon through the muskeg so the trail does not turn into a
ditch the first wet October, and tent platforms where a party would actually want to stop.

## What it opens

Three cabins on boot leather instead of two hours of hoping for a window. A way out that
does not need a floatplane. And goat country above *Patching Lake* that hardly anybody walks
into, because right now getting there means chartering in and hunting from the water.

The line on the map is pink and it says *not surveyed*, because it is not. That will change in
that order: walked, studied, then cut.
