---
title: Bringing SEAtrails back starts in the towns
date: 2026-10-03
excerpt: Nineteen communities built the regional trail system. The organization went quiet, and the old domain no longer describes the trails. Ketchikan is where this map starts.
---

SEAtrails began in 2000 as a deal between Southeast towns and the ferry that connects them.
Each community picked the trails it wanted on the regional map. A traveler could step off the
Alaska Marine Highway and find a route somebody local had already stood behind. Ketchikan was
one of those towns, and the archive calls it the connection point for Prince of Wales.

The record of that system is still online at [akseatrails.org](https://akseatrails.org/).
Southeast Conference took custody of the old materials in 2022 and has said it is looking for
funding to revive a regional approach. That is their work. This group is not Southeast
Conference, and we are not the old nonprofit.

What we can do is the part the original idea actually depended on. People in a town decide
which trails are real, which ones are brushed over, and which gaps are worth a survey. On
Revilla that work is the map. Five of the six Ketchikan routes the old guide named are already
on it. The sixth is an underwater trail at Mountain Point, which a foot-trail map is the wrong
tool for.

The hostname people remember, seatrails.org, is a different matter. Checked October 3, 2026,
it serves an unrelated commercial site. We do not link it. If you are looking for the trails,
use the archive, or [start with the Ketchikan piece](/seatrails).

If you live in one of the other eighteen communities that passed a resolution when the system
was active, write to us. A resolution from your assembly or council was the old first step,
and the template is still on the archive. A mapped network of what you can actually walk is
the useful second step. We will help a town think that through. We will not pretend a line is
surveyed, and we will not put a town on this site as a partner until the people who live there
say so.

Ketchikan's resolution is history. It is not a blank check for this group. The ranger district
has not signed off on a new trail, and neither has the borough. The map says who owns the
ground so those conversations can happen in the open.
