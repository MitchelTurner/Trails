The work of bringing SEAtrails back needed a front door. This is it. The name on the site is
SEAtrails. The people publishing it are an informal group. There is no new corporation, and
this is not the old 501(c)(3). Southeast Conference still holds the materials it acquired in
2022. The nineteen towns that passed resolutions have not passed new ones.

The map we can defend is still the Ketchikan piece, on Revillagigedo Island, because that is
the ground this group walks. Other towns get a page when residents there are doing the work.

Students — high school and college — can [join the youth crew](/get-involved#youth) and
volunteer on construction: brushing tread, walking a flagged line, learning why a survey comes
before a cut. Under 18, a parent or guardian signs up with you. A chainsaw stays with someone
who holds a current saw card.

We are asking universities to put a [scholarship or an educational certificate](/universities)
on that work. University of Alaska Southeast already has campuses in Ketchikan, Sitka, and
Juneau, and any other campus can write. Naming them is an invitation. No campus has agreed,
no amount has been set, and this group will not print a certificate and call it a university
credential. A scholarship, if a campus creates one, is awarded to the student. We cannot take
the money. There is no entity to hold it.

The basic facts — name, status, email, archive, and where the paperwork stands — are on the
[organization page](/about). The hostname people remember, seatrails.org, still serves an
unrelated commercial site. Checked October 3, 2026. We do not link it. The trail archive
remains [akseatrails.org](https://akseatrails.org/).
